## 02-business-idea-and-usp.adoc

**2.1** The idea:
> Dani Milli Studio has created original illustration and surface pattern design since 2016, inspired by stories, nature and the beauty of repetition. The business idea joins two markets that are separate today: relaxing, artistic games and apps for movement and focus. The game is worth playing on its own. Players who want to can let real movement or focus time drive progress in the game world. Revenue is planned from optional purchases that give no gameplay advantage, such as visual themes and new areas of the game world. The exact model isn't decided yet. Licensing to wellness and education is a possibility in the long term.

**2.2** Target customer:
> *Art and products:* consumers in Europe buying gifts and everyday products with original art; entrepreneurs, small businesses and brands who want to license patterns for their products; clients who need illustrations, patterns or story-driven concepts.
>
> *Tessera:* players who already enjoy relaxing exploration games with a strong artistic identity, such as Sky or Alto's Odyssey. Also people who use habit or fitness apps but feel them as an obligation rather than something fun. Players fall into three groups: game-first, combined-value and lifestyle-first. Tessera needs only the first two to be viable. It doesn't target people who only want a fitness app.

**2.3** Customer need:
> *Art and products:* customers want everyday objects and gifts that carry original, story-driven art. Businesses want engaging patterns that make their products stand out.
>
> *Tessera:* many people want to relax with screen time that feels meaningful, not passive scrolling. Research on players of relaxing games shows they mainly want freedom to play at their own pace, a break from everyday life, moments that move them, a story, and a sense that the time is well spent. Tessera builds on this: the art creates moments of wonder, and the story is told through how the world changes as the player restores it. Fitness and habit apps, meanwhile, often stop feeling fun over time. Tessera is designed to solve both: an artistic, relaxing game first, with an optional link to real-world habits as a bonus, not a requirement.

**2.4** New subsection, "How the need was identified":
> Dani then examined whether others share the need. That work had two parts. The first was a literature review of research on recovery, relaxing games, player motives, the role of art and story in games, and habit formation. The second was an analysis of comparable products: some where art is central to the experience (Sky: Children of the Light, Alto's Odyssey) and, separately, habit-focused products (Habitica, Finch, and StepN, which collapsed). This rests on research and competitor analysis. Testing with the target group will use playable versions of the game.

**2.5** The offer:
> *Art and products:* original illustration and surface pattern designs printed on phone accessories, mousepads, bags, frames, notebooks, wrapping paper, magnets, mugs, bento boxes, mason jars and tea towels. > *Tessera:* a stylized 3D mobile exploration game set across fractured worlds, the Tesserae, hit by an unexplained Void. Art is a core pillar: it makes the world worth being in, creates moments of awe, and tells the story through how the world changes. Real walking or focus time can feed progress, but ordinary play stays worthwhile without any real-world task.

**2.6** Why buy from this studio:
