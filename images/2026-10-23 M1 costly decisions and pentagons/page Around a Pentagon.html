Around a Pentagon
Tessera M1 · round world, planet size 12

Why crates and walls keep clear of the pentagons

The planet has 12 pentagons, and nothing stands on them. The tiles right around a pentagon are also different: they are smaller, and they bend round it. These two patches come from the real planet in the trial, drawn flat at the same scale.

Ring by ring

A ring is the band of tiles at the same number of steps from the pentagon. Bend is how much a tile's north–south line turns compared with its neighbours'.

Why it matters

  • Crates move in a straight line, a half-tile step at a time, and each step must end on a tile's middle or on the line between two tiles. Where the tiles bend, the steps end part way across a tile, so the crate comes to rest in odd places or gets stuck.
  • Walls are one stone block per tile. Beside a pentagon, a straight wall comes out crooked, with blocks of different widths.
  • Everything else is fine there: plain ground, grass, water, trees, rocks and things to gather. None of them need straight rows.

My recommendation

Keep crates, walls and buildings off the pentagon and its first two rings. That is 16 tiles round each pentagon: 192 of the planet's 1,442 tiles, or 13%. From the third ring on, tiles are close to normal.

Today's island is well clear: its middle lies 8 rings from the nearest pentagon.

Measured on the trial's planet at size 12, with 3.75 m tiles. The pentagons lie 12 tiles apart. The bend figures are averages, taken round a pentagon on the equator.
