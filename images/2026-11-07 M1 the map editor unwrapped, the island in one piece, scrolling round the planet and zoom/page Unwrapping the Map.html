Unwrapping the map

How the map editor could show the round planet flat, the way a 3D artist unwraps a model before painting it.

A cup unrolls. A ball doesn’t.

Cut a cup’s side once, along the red line, and it rolls out flat. No square stretches. A ball can’t do that. Its curve sits in its 12 pentagons, and each pentagon needs a cut of its own.

Every flat map of a ball has to choose: cut it, or stretch it. 3D artists cut. Today’s editor stretches.

Today: stretched

Your island Empty tiles Pentagon How much wider tiles are drawn

The editor flattens the whole planet round one tile, 6,4. Nothing is cut, so tiles get wider the farther they are from 6,4. Of your 263 tiles, 64 are drawn within 5% of their real shape. 89 are drawn more than 20% too wide, and 24,5, at the east end, is 54% too wide.

The plan: unwrapped

Your island Pentagon Cut open: no tiles here Edge of a cut These two tiles touch in the game

Every tile is drawn as the same hexagon, like squares on graph paper, so none stretch. Behind each pentagon the map is cut open, the way a 3D artist cuts seams. The red edges of a cut are joined in the game: walk across the gap and you step straight onto the tile on the other side.

Your tile names already work this way. Each column,row is one slot on this flat honeycomb, and the slots in a cut have no tile. So the map file, the names and the saves stay as they are. Only the editor’s picture changes.

What you’d notice

  • A path, pond or wall that crosses a cut looks broken in the editor but is joined in the game.
  • A pentagon is drawn as a hexagon with one side on a cut.
  • Near a pentagon, the real tiles in the game are smaller than the editor shows: a quarter narrower right beside it, about 5% narrower four tiles away. Seven or more tiles away, they match within 1%.

To decide

Where the cuts go

Each cut starts at its pentagon and runs away from 6,4. Four cuts reach your island: 21 pairs of your tiles touch in the game but sit on opposite sides of a cut, 11 of them by 10,10. A 3D artist hides seams where no one looks. The cuts could be turned toward the void instead, but that renames every tile beyond them, so your map would need converting once.

Recommendation: keep the cuts where they are at first. Only the picture changes, and nothing in your map is renamed. Move a cut later only if it gets in your way while you build.
