Building floating tiles

My idea for the map editor, in pictures. Nothing here changes the game.

The whole picture

  1. Every tile has a height, in 0.5 m steps. There are no hill tiles. Click a tile and its box has a Height slider; the Height tool changes many tiles quickly, a click for each step.
  2. Base tiles are columns up from the core, from a pond's bed at −2 m to 20 m. Their bottoms fade into the void. There is no lid.
  3. A hidden base tile waits low in the void. When it's unlocked, it grows up to its height and carries the player up with it.
  4. Unhappy, then grass: a tile is grey with no grass until the green reaches it.
  5. Water tiles sit on base tiles. The base tile's top is the pond's bed. Touching water shares one level, with no faces between. You walk into water: shallow, you walk; deep, you swim. Stepping stones stay.
  6. Floating tiles sit in layers. A floating tile's bottom is at its layer, and its height is how tall it is, from 0.5 m up. No shared thickness. They go unhappy, then grass, with the tile under them.
  7. On floating tiles you can put plants, harvestables, rocks, chests, stone blocks, walls, key items, machines, lanterns and diamonds.
  8. Clouds lie round the outer edges of grass tiles. Their height is a slider you set afterwards.
  9. The editor: a layer row above the map (Ground, 4 m, 8 m, + Add). Every tool works on the picked layer. The Floating tiles tool adds and removes floating tiles.

What you build: layers, like the floors of a building

A layer is one height. Every floating tile at that height belongs to it. Here the island has two layers: three tiles at 4 m (the player stands on one) and two at 8 m.

Unhappy, then grass

A floating tile is a real tile, like the ones on the ground. It is unhappy until the green reaches it, then it turns to grass, at the same moment as the tile under it. Today a floating tile's top is white instead.

Before the green reaches it: grey, no grass.

After: grass on top and down its sides, soil under it.

How you build it in the editor

1Pick Floating tiles, then the 4 m layer. Click tiles to add them. Click one again to remove it.
2Pick 8 m. Now clicks build the 8 m layer. The 4 m tiles go faint, so you still see where they are.
3Pick any other tool, like Grass. Floating tiles show as dashed outlines with their height inside.

While you build, the ground goes pale, so the floating tiles stand out. Layer at moves the picked layer, all its tiles together. + Add makes a new layer.

Putting things on them

Key items already work. The game puts a key item on the highest place on its tile that you can walk on. Place one on a tile with a floating tile over it, and it lies on the floating tile.

For everything else, the layer row moves above the map, with Ground first, and every tool works on the layer you pick. Pick 4 m and Puffball tree, click a floating tile, and the tree stands on it. Pick Ground, and tools work on the ground as they do now.

Height: one tool for every tile

No hill tiles. Every tile, on the ground or floating, has a height, and the Height tool changes it. Each click raises a tile 0.5 m; a right-click lowers it. With Ground picked, it changes base tiles, from a pond's bed at −2 m up to 20 m.

Why one height and not hill tiles: your ponds already need base tiles to go down (a bed at −2 m), so base tiles get a height anyway. A hill is the same number going up. With hill tiles, each raised tile would be two things, the base and the hill on it, which must always move together, for example when the tile grows out of the void.

On floating tiles too. A floating tile's bottom is at its layer, and its height is how tall it is, from 0.5 m up. Pick a layer, then the Height tool, and each click makes one floating tile 0.5 m taller. Its bottom stays put, so the room to walk under it doesn't change. The map shows how tall each one is.
