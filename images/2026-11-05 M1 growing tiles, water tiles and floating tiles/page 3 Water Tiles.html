Water tiles

Your idea: water is a tile placed on top of another tile's column. Where two water tiles touch, there's no face between them.

Yes, it's better

  • One rule for every tile. A column of a height, and optionally water on top of it. No separate water object with rules of its own.
  • Ponds at any height. Water on a 2 m column is a pond on a hill.
  • Depth per tile. The column under the water is the bed, so one pond can be deep in the middle and shallow at its edge.
  • The look stays the same. The game already builds the water like this inside: one top over all its tiles, with faces only where the tile beside isn't water. What changes is where its levels come from.

Seen from the side

Each water tile is a column (its bed, at the tile's height) with water on top up to the water's level. Both are set in 0.5 m steps.

Seen from above

Three water tiles at one level, the tiles in front of them missing. One top, faces only on the outside, none where water tiles meet. Their beds are at different depths.

Two water tiles at different levels: the back one at −0.5 m, the front one at −1 m. The back one shows a face above the front one's top, a step in the water.

Two things to decide

Q7 · walkable

I read "walkable" as: where the water is up to 0.5 m deep, you walk on the tile under it, and where it's deeper, you swim. That brings shallow water back in a new form (you removed shallow water on 4 November). I recommend this, with the depth as a slider.

Q8 · levels

Water tiles that touch share one level, set together in the editor, so there are never steps like the right-hand picture. I recommend this. Steps can come later if you want waterfalls.

This replaces Q5: a water tile gets its own height (its bed) and its water's level, so ponds can sit anywhere.
